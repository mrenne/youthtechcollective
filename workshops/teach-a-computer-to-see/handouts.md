# Teach a Computer to See — Handouts

Print the challenge briefs on cardstock and cut them apart. The take-home sheet and exit survey are each sized for one page.

---

## A. Challenge briefs (Build 2)

Every brief has the same deliverables: **2–4 classes + a "nothing" class**, a **10-try accuracy score** tested by another pair, and a one-sentence answer to *"What happens if it gets this wrong?"*

**1. Smart Recycling Sorter**
*User:* a school cafeteria. Sort *paper*, *plastic* and *metal* (use the demo bag). Which mistake is worse: plastic in paper, or paper in plastic?

**2. Hands-Free Controls**
*User:* someone who can't easily use a mouse or keyboard. Design 3 gestures for "next," "back" and "select." How do you stop it triggering by accident?

**3. Doodle Reader**
*User:* a drawing game. Recognize a star, a heart and a lightning bolt drawn on paper. Does it work on doodles by people who weren't in your training data?

**4. Sound Alert (audio project)**
*User:* a Deaf or hard-of-hearing person at home. Detect a doorbell, a knock and a timer beep (play them from a phone). Which matters more here: avoiding false alarms or never missing an alert?

**5. Posture Checker (pose project)**
*User:* a gamer or student who sits for hours. Tell "good posture" from "slouching." Does it work for both partners, who are different heights?

**6. Shelf Scanner**
*User:* a library or store. Recognize 3 different book covers or product boxes. What happens with a cover it has never seen?

**7. Pitch Your Own**
Got a better idea? Pitch it to the facilitator in one sentence: *who* it's for and *what* it recognizes. Objects, gestures, drawings, sounds and poses are all fair game. Sorting people by how they look is not.

## B. Take-home sheet

### 🤖 You trained an AI today!

**What you learned**
- AI learns from **examples** (training data), not from rules.
- **Bad data makes bad AI.** If the examples are missing something, the AI gets it wrong.
- You can build this yourself, for free.

**Try it at home**
Go to **teachablemachine.withgoogle.com**, click *Get Started*, and choose:
- **Image Project**: teach it to recognize things on camera (what you did today)
- **Audio Project**: teach it to recognize sounds, like claps, whistles or words
- **Pose Project**: teach it to recognize body poses, like dance moves or yoga poses

**Challenges to try**
1. Build a model that works for *everyone* in your household, not just you. How many training images did it take?
2. Train a pose model that counts reps of an exercise.
3. Export a model and use it on a webpage with the free p5.js sample code from Teachable Machine.

**Keep going**
- micro:bit CreateAI (createai.microbit.org): train AI on movement and run it on a micro:bit
- Machine Learning for Kids (machinelearningforkids.co.uk): connect AI models to Scratch and Python projects
- Ask your librarian about the next workshop!

*Parents and caregivers: Teachable Machine is a free Google tool that runs in your web browser. In its default mode, training images stay on your device. No account is needed to try it.*
